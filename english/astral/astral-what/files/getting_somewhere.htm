Getting Somewhere With Astral Projection
By: Emthren

Learning To Learn

"Each projection attempt makes me feel like Alice. With two halves of a mushroom, I grow and shrink to get through a series of different doors, until I come to the heart of Wonderland."

By this day-and-age, there's a lot of information "out there" on astral projection. From TV shows, to books, to articles in magazines or scientific journals, there are resources enough to saturate a person's mind. And that is without considering the realm of the Internet - where anybody can put up there 2 cents on the topic, and expose it to literally thousands of people.

With all these resources, it begs the question why are people still having difficulty projecting?
One could argue that there isn't enough new information out there - most websites rehash what has already been said, put it on a starry background, and call it information. Perhaps. While it might happen, occasionally some new insight and progress is made in the world of theory.

Similarly, I doubt it's due to a lack of methods or techniques. There's only so many ways you can say "relax your body" or "calm your mind". The visualisations might differ, but many methods are quite similar. I wouldn't go so far as to say "read one, read them all". That's definitely not the case - for example, Robert Bruce's ROPE technique has something, the idea of tactile rather than visual imagination, that many methods don't present. Same goes for Robert Peterson's swaying cube and the idea of induced motion. There is substantial variation, but there are still certain commonalities.

So if it's not a lack of information, what is it? I'd argue it's a question of attitude - the idea of learning to learn.

Variation between people's projection experiences is practically infinite.
Some people get sleep paralysis, some don't.
Some get vibrations, some don't.
Some feel fear, some don't.
Some meet their guide, some don't.
Some feel energy going through them, some don't.
And so on, regarding just about every possible factor.

The bottom line is that people's experiences, and therefore requirements, significantly vary. And to complicate matters further, it seems that the requirements for the same person change over time as well - or even 'worse', between nights.

It's not so much a case of learning a particular method and doing it well, rather guessing where you want to be, and trying out things to get you there. The analogy of astral projection to exploring is highly relevant in this regard. For all the commonalities that people experience, your own mind is unique, and nobody else's map is going to cover it. You have to forge ahead and work out what you need to accomplish to get where you want to be. It's all about "you", I'd doubt anyone has the authority to say "do this now to get that". There's gates to cross, and you have to make the keys yourself. Enough with the analogies though.

If it's all about you, the idea of a message board to discuss astral projection seems pointless, no?
No. While everyone has their own way, like I said previously there are commonalities. In some cases, it might not be necessary to reinvent the wheel. If someone has had a similar problem or issue to yourself, it is quite beneficial to use their solution (if there is one). But, while someone else might provide a solution, it might not necessarily be the particular solution that's relevant for you. Again, it comes down to your own experience - you decide what's right for you by trying it out repeatedly.

With all this talk of it coming down to the individual, it does seem a bit disheartening. It's not that bad however, unless you came looking for a "quick fix". As with most things though, there is no easy way and work is the way to get there.

So what work is needed?
To be a little more upbeat, think of it as play. Or better, yet, experimentation. Try this way tonight. Try that way tomorrow night. Try no way the next night, and see if anything happens, and note the results you get.

Try to keep some patience about you, and don't worry about screwing up the experience. Invariably you might learn something completely new by doing something that might not make complete sense (e.g. rolling over when relaxed, to find your astral body rolling rather than physical). Play and experiment - being a little less serious about the whole affair can do a world of difference.

Back to the idea of exploring though, I believe the key to gaining the skill of consistent projection is by acquainted yourself with all the different stages of projection. From the second your eyes close, if you can comfortably slip from stage to stage, recognising them from past experiences, you're well on your way. And when you get "stuck", again play around to see what gets you closer to the next stage.

A cautionary note though, I don't mean to imply that the process of astral projection is marked by clear and distinct stages. In my experience, I do use some broad labels such as "body relaxed", "mind relaxed", "sleep paralysis", "hypnagogic imagery", but by no means are they set and definite. Like was said earlier, they change on a night by night basis, and have no clear borders. Sometimes I'll miss one altogether, sometimes I'll get two at once.

By gaining this deep familiarity with each stage, a sense of exploration of the next stage can begin from a solid base. And by repeating this enough, eventually the next stage will be a complete conscious projection, and who knows what the possibilities are beyond that…
