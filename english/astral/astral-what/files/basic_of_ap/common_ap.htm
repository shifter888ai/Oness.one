Common AP/OBE Phenomena

Introduction

A number of sensations and events are common to people who attempt astral projection (AP) or out-of-body experiences (OBE). Although the similarities are clear, they are by no means definite. It is just rare to find an individual who experiences all of these sensations as it is to find someone who experiences none of them.

Individual variations also occur, where the intensity of some sensations can vary from person to person, or people may simply not notice some sensations occurring. Variations are not just limited between people. Each projection attempt may yield different phenomena, with different intensity, in a different sequence. Although these phenomena are listed in a general sequence of how they are often experienced, it is by no means a rigid list.

Finally, these phenomena can be used as an goals to aim for, however, one can get into the trap of focusing on attaining something that isn't necessary to them. It is perhaps better to have AP/OBE as the one goal, and use these sensations as rough confirmations that the right track is being followed.

Weight changes

Sudden feelings that the body is exceedingly heavy or very light both can be used to indicate a significant relaxed state has been achieved. A heavy body both signifies that the body is relaxed and no muscle tension is occurring. Also, the commonly ignored sensations of the pressure of your body on whatever surface it's resting on comes to the conscious mind. Similarly, a light-weight feeling can occur as the mind further disengages from the body, causing the heaviness to vanish completely.

Stopping breathing

Apparent shortness of breath, or feelings of not breathing altogether, can lead to panic and outright terror. However, the cause is relatively simple and straightforward. As the mind falls further, it is not a person's breathing that stops, but the *awareness* of breathing that is reduced. Trusting your automatic nervous system to continue breathing, even if you can't feel it, is the workaround.

Temperature changes

Also associated with the process of falling asleep is a slight reduction in body temperature. For this reason it's a good idea to cover up a little more than necessary before projection.

Conscious sleep paralysis

Typical reports of conscious sleep paralysis describe how a person can't move their body or speak. After a short period of attempting to struggle to move, the person can slowly start to move. This may occur when a person is both falling asleep, or when they are waking up. Despite the intense fear that conscious sleep paralysis can create, sleep paralysis is a relatively normal process. When a person enters into REM sleep, the body is paralysed to prevent it acting out dreamed events, and possibly causing injury.

In regards to AP/OBE, conscious sleep paralysis when falling asleep is a relatively good indicator that one is entering the body asleep/mind awake stage of consciousness. To attain this state, simple relaxation with a subsequent deepening on the relaxed state is all that's needed.

Hypnagogic imagery

These hallucinations (perceptions without an appropriate stimulus) are mostly visual and auditory. Visual examples may include geometric shapes or various coloured lights. Auditory hallucinations can include the sound of bells ringing, cracks, whistles or pops, and even music and speech. Probably caused by an overlap of dreaming and waking, they are still hallucinations and nothing to fear. The greatest danger is that one is startled by the sounds and taken out of the relaxed state.

Infinitely large/small feeling

Changes to a person's perception of themselves can occur. Either a person may feel as if they're shrinking into an infinitesimal point of consciousness, or growing and expanding until they reach the edges of the Universe.

The growing or shrinking feeling usually happens after sleep paralysis has set in, thus leading to the conclusion that the sensation is probably caused by a mind/body detachment. The physical limits of the body no longer apply, since as far as the mind is concerned, the body is no longer.

Both the pin-pointed consciousness and expanded consciousness feelings can be used as methods to AP/OBE. If pin-pointed, willing the consciousness to move in any direction often results in projection. To project from an expanded feeling, attempting to roll over may bring results.

Heart racing

A racing heart, to the point where it feels like it's about to jump out of your chest, has two possible explanations.

Firstly, it may simply be the awareness of the heart beat shifting, so something that was once ignored suddenly becomes consciously experienced. Secondly, it may seem to be a side-effect of fear. Especially when many of the sensations occur the first few times, they are new and completely beyond all previous experience. Similarly to this, the very onset of vibrations may trigger an adrenaline response (a lot like going over the first big hill in a roller-coaster).

Another popular way of seeing the racing-heart phenomenon is as the heart chakra opening up and providing the astral body with enough energy to separate from the physical one.

Either case, remaining passive and calm and waiting for your heart rate to subside should allow you to continue with your projection attempt. Also gaining familiarity with the different states of consciousness may help with the fear response.

Vibrations

Vibrations are an extremely common occurrence, however, they are not necessary for projection. Some people report never experiencing them, others report the vibrations diminishing as they gain experience projecting. Weather one looses the vibrations themselves, or simply doesn't overlook them, is another matter.

Despite the term "vibrations", there are also a few different ways they can be felt. Firstly, it has been described like electricity ripping through a person's body, slowly ripping them apart. The second type is a surge/rushing/falling feeling, comparable to that like on a roller coaster. Of course, variations on these two styles, or mixes of the two, also occur. It is likely every person's experience is different, as are their ways of describing the vibrations.

What causes these vibrations still appears to be a topic of debate. Theories run from somatosensory hallucinations, to the flow of energy through chakras or the astral body attempting to wiggle out of the physical body. Whatever the cause, it's generally held as one of the last experiences before projection.

To confuse matters further, there are "false vibrations", which is a product of muscles relaxing. These vibrations are comparatively weak. Similarly, feelings of the body expanding and contracting may also be taken for vibrations. Neither of these contain the initial intensity of the vibrations.

Blackout

Once the vibrations reach a seemingly unbearable peak, some people find themselves suddenly loosing consciousness for a few seconds. When it returns, the person is in what's commonly described as a "void". From here, any scene may be created.
