Hi Tom,
doing these cycles by own understanding and without a dogmatic master is a good way. An dogmatic master certainly would teach you quite a lot of unnecessary traditions (what would be distracting from real, important features). A very important aspect, at least in Buddhist tradition (I am conform with) is ethic and spirituality, but you can develop this as well without a teacher.

Now to the theory: You have two processes of energy transformation:

1) Transforming body vitality (sexual energy) into chi.
Body vitality (especial sexual energy) which is situated in the abdomen (the Chinese relate this energy to the kidneys) has to be transformed into chi, a kind of etheric energy. Associated with alchemy (the Chinese like to illustrate and associate the processes with different philosophic and other systems) it is spoken about the transformation of water (sexual energy, body vitality) into steam (symbol for the more subtle form of water and thus for the product of transformation of water = chi, by the indian yogis it is called prana). Thus the sexual energy is transformed (by means of the small circle) by the fire of the heart (emotions) into steam (chi). In your imagination you are circling energy from the chest downwards to the base and again upwards to the chest (to give attention to the five hollow organs, as they are called - stomach, liver etc., is not necessary, it is more an aspect of medicine than an aspect of spirituality). This cycle is similar to the process of a steam driven engine, - in a continual way, water from the abdomen has to be transported to the fire of the heart (transport is done by imagination), where the water of vitality is transformed to etheric energy (chi, prana). It needs some time, until the blockades of the channels (inner meridians, ind. yoga = nadis) have been removed, so that the energies indeed can circle. For that reason it is recommended to practice the small circle of energy for a certain time. In common, if you feel that your spine becomes hot, the subtle form of energy (chi) is produced in an agreeable amount, so that you can start the second process of transformation (meditating at the great cycle).
If you have come as far you start your daily meditations for a short while with the small cycle and continue afterwards with the great heavenly cycle alone (after a short "warming up period" you let drop the small cycle and perform only the great heavenly cycle as explained below!).

2) Transforming chi into spiritual energy (forming the embryo of the immortal light body)
In the great circle chi is transported upwards the spine (by means of imagination = meditation) and into the head and there to the forehead (ind. front Chakra, Ajna Chakra) the source of light (it is meant light as quality, but not light as light-substance, as it is needed to form the immortal embryo). In the head there is further the heavenly lake of ambrosia (in ind. yoga it is called amrita), the source of life energy - this nectar also is mixed with the new product of chi transformation, the liquid light (above I have called it substantial light).

To sustain meditation the eye balls are rolled (circled) as well - therefore the Taoistic heroes have very great and red eye balls in the Chinese and Japanese paintings.

From the forehead the liquid of golden light is transported downwards the front side of the body ( Jenn Mo meridian) to the belly where it is stored and accumulated thus making the embryo of light to grow (it resembles the process in the womb of a mother when she gets a baby). (In Chinese and Japanese paintings the Buddhas and heroes have a great belly, indicating that they have fulfilled a successful transformation). When the embryo of light has grown up to a body of light the process is finished. The Taoist can shift his consciousness into the body of light and leave the physical body with his immortal Boddhisattwa body (in China very often Taoism is mixed with Buddhism, and both are using this now explained method), to help other people, to enter the heaven and so on.
