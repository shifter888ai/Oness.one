2: The Long Fight for Acceptance

Micro-PK experiments -- examining the ability of human consciousness to affect minute influences on machines -- are a relatively recent line of research, having appeared only 30 years ago. Studying the power of the human consciousness, however, has been a tradition since the dawn of humankind. Margins of Reality traces a long line of mystical and parapsychological inquiry by scientists including Aristotle, Newton, Pauli, and Einstein. The authors rightly ask the question: If these scientists broached the subject, why does the scientific community as a whole typically shy from examining the relationship between consciousness and action, dream and reality, spirit and body?

Einstein argued that scientific skepticism springs from the artificial labels, categories, and theories we attach to natural processes: "Concepts which have been proved to be useful in ordering things easily acquire such an authority over us that we forget their human origin and accept them as invariable."

But the system of concepts, he says, is merely a creation of the human mind, a freely chosen convention, "just as is the case with the concept of causality."

One scientist who did not shy away was German-born physicist Helmut Schmidt. Working at the Institute of Parapsychology (also known as the Rhine Research Center) in Durham, North Carolina, in the early 1970s, Schmidt was among the first to study the effects of human consciousness on REGs.

Typical of Schmidt's machines was a device with two lights, one red and one green. Operators would attempt to make the red light come on more often than the green light, solely by the power of their thoughts. Throughout Schmidt's testing, the operators' success rate was only one or two percent above the machine's random output.

Schmidt's articles, published in the Journal of Parapsychology, allowed others to duplicate his experiments. From the mid-1950s to the present, at least 75 independent labs have conducted hundreds of experiments on the powers of human consciousness. Their methods have improved, with recent experiments following strict scientific methodology under repeatable conditions. And their findings have all been similar: Operators have made small but statistically significant and recurring alterations to REGs by means of their intention alone.

"Schmidt and those who followed him caused a fundamental shift in how we perceive psychokinesis," said the Institute of Parapsychology's Dr. Richard Broughton. "In the late 1800s, PK experiments involved moving tables or objects. When we moved into micro-PK, we found that human consciousness is biasing nature's random processes."
