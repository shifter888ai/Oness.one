Yoga Sutra of Patanjali

 

Translation by Mark Giubarelli

 

Patanjali was a great sage that lived thousands of years ago. The yoga Sutras he wrote seem to have transcended time remaining one of the most influential spiritual writings in Yoga. The Yoga Sutras consist of 195 verses divided into 4 chapters of divine spiritual wisdom. They contain the knowledge and instructions that have inspired Yoga practitioners for centuries.

 

Chapter I - State of Union

Chapter II - Practice

Chapter III - Powers

Chapter IV – Emancipation

 

 

1. State of Union

1.1.   Now begins the sacred instructions of union.

1.2.   Union by stopping mind activity.

1.3.   At this time the seer enters their own true state.

1.4.   At other times the mind fluctuates and the seer identifies with this. The seer is then elsewhere.

1.5.   There are 5 fluctuations. They may affect us positively or negatively.

1.6.   Correct perception, incorrect perception , imagination, sleep and memory.

1.7.   Correct perception is derived from direct experience, spiritual text, inference or proven facts.

1.8.   Incorrect perception is illusion based on false beliefs that have been observed, accepted, and embraced.

1.9.   Verbal knowledge that lacks substance is from imagination.

1.10.                    There is an absence of awareness in sleep. Condition the mind for sleep without dreams. This is deep sleep.

1.11.                    The retention of past perceptions, experiences and knowlege is memory

1.12.                    Practice regularly to control desires and eventually become freed from them.

1.13.                    Practice is the continued effort to still the re-occuring mind fluctuations.

1.14.                    Practice for a long time with devotion and enthusiasm.

1.15.                    Freedom from desires is achieved with intellect and control.

1.16.                    Higher states of self are perceived and understood. Distractions disappear.

1.17.                    Known is considered then understood. Then comes bliss from the true knowledge.

1.18.                    When in Union there is stillness but past impressions may arise. Memory in time is balanced to serve us in life but not lead us astray.

1.19.                    What arises is a state of awareness where the seer is merged with nature.

1.20.                    Previous knowledge and memory serve the cause. Faith, strength and profound meditation lead us further.

1.21.                    Union is near for those with faith and vigor.

1.22.                    The results vary depending on intensity and dedication toward union. There is a mild, medium and intense approach.

1.23.                    Complete surrender to God in profound meditation brings union.

1.24.                    God is complete and unaffected by affliction, suffering and karma.

1.25.                    From God comes the seed of all knowledge.

1.26.                    God is the teacher of all teachers through time.

1.27.                    God becomes present in the sacred sound of Aum.

1.28.                    This is the purpose of repeating the sacred sound.

1.29.                    Then the soul and inner self can be found. An intervention can take place to further remove obstacles from the path.

1.30.                    Obstacles such as disease, doubt, illusion, inability to understand, laziness, gratification of the senses, slipping from progress and a scattered mind are all impediments.

1.31.                    Other distractions are sorrow, Despair, anxiety, improper breathing and an unfocused mind.

1.32.                    To prevent this follow a devotional one pointed pratice.

1.33.                    A peaceful tranquility surfaces when we encourage joy, happiness and virtue. Detach from pain, sorrow, vice and suffering.

1.34.                    After exhalation, during the stillness before inhalation, there is calmness. This can remove distractions.

1.35.                    Or by unifying the mind with an object.

1.36.                    Or by removing all sorrow and meditating on a shining state of illumination within.

1.37.                    Or by contemplating sages.

1.38.                    Or by inquiry into day dreaming, dreams from sleep and states of awakening.

1.39.                    Or by meditating upon something of our liking that is in harmony with the path.

1.40.                    When progressed the mind will understand all there is to be known. From the smallest particle to that which is vast and distant.

1.41.                    When controlling fluctuations is mastered the seer becomes one with the seen. The seen is known and shines forth through the seer like the suns reflection on a flawless crystal.

1.42.                    Words, purpose, intelligence and imagination merge and transform.

1.43.                    Memory becomes pure and untainted by nature. Pure form exists without reflection.

1.44.                    Reflection may or may not be used to comprehend the subtle bodies.

1.45.                    The subtle bodies are without form.

1.46.                    These previous forms of union are described with seeds.

1.47.                    A skillful practitioner that has grown aware of the relationship between divinity and the self may meditate without the use of seeds and reflection. They shine without fluctuation.

1.48.                    The essence of truth causes insight and pure wisdom.

1.49.                    This knowledge is not based on memory. It is beyond that which has been attained from external sources.

1.50.                    A new life of spiritual wisdom manifests ,old impression that may arise and cause conflict are left , new ones are prevented.

1.51.                    With the restraint and continual effort on transformation the seedless states of profound meditation and absolute union occur.

 

2.      Practice

2.1.   With burning desire reflect upon and understand the self, understand the sacred text and surrender all actions to God.

2.2.   Meditate profoundly on becoming completely absorbed to reduce afflictions.

2.3.   Afflictions causing disturbance are lack of spiritual awareness, misapprehension, ego, desire, passion, greed, hate, jealousy, attachment to life, fear, anxiety and sorrow.

2.4.   Some afflictions and their source remain hidden waiting to be awakened. This is due to a lack of spiritual awareness and ignorance. Others afflictions have bloomed and are fully active.

2.5.   The need for pleasures and the source of suffering comes from identifying with that which is impure and not eternal. True self is pure and eternal.

2.6.   Perception and thought intertwine to create ego.

2.7.   Pleasure is seductive and causes deep attachments.

2.8.   Hatred and resentment are related to fear and suffering.

2.9.   Even the wise and spiritually progressed have attachment to life.

2.10.                    For progress these afflictions and obstacles must be removed. No matter how small they may be.

2.11.                    Meditation removes these disturbances.

2.12.                    It may be possible to see the cause of suffering, but the karma may also be hidden, unrelated to time and the life being experienced.

2.13.                    Our very being is rooted in karma. It affects the place of birth, quality of life and its span.

2.14.                    We experience joy or suffering and vice or virtue as a result of Karma.

2.15.                    With awareness and wisdom of thought discrimination, rising impressions of distress, pain and suffering can suppressed before they manifest.

2.16.                    Then future suffering can also be prevented.

2.17.                    Pain comes from the inability to separate true self from the illusory self.

2.18.                    Light shines on nature revealing 3 qualities. Its ability to absorb and reflect light. Its vibration and constant change and its shape and density. This and the elements are perceived through the five senses, engaged with the five organs of action and considered with the mind. Nature's pleasures can seduce the seer unless the seer remains disengaged for the sake of liberation and freedom.

2.19.                    It may or may not be apparent but nature's 3 qualities and all that is seen is in continual change. A greater intelligence may be seen in everything creating a perception of unity in all that is.

2.20.                    Awareness becomes pure when this perception of unity in nature is developed.

2.21.                    For this one reason all that is seen must be perceived by the true self.

2.22.                    Those who attain this perception of unity in nature are no longer bound to it. All others are.

2.23.                    Perception of unity in nature and the true self merge creating a force that frees us from the needs and bondage's of life. One becomes a master of ones self, no longer a slave to nature.

2.24.                    A lack of true awareness will keep us bound to the needs and cravings of nature.

2.25.                    By severing the bonds of nature and remaining in union one reaches absolute freedom and emancipation.

2.26.                    One must continually separate truth from illusion.

2.27.                    There are seven aspects that must be practiced before we reach a state of absolute truth.

2.28.                    With devoted practice in these aspects of Yoga, impurities and obstacles are eliminated and true wisdom in its essence shines.

2.29.                    These seven aspects are self restraint, universal laws, sacred posture, regulation of breath, control and restraint of the senses, concentration and meditation. With their practice comes the eighth aspect of Yoga. This is a state of being absolutely absorbed in the source of life.

2.30.                    In the practice of the first aspect the following is considered. Non-violence, honesty, non-stealing, celibacy, study of scripture and not possessing or consuming that which is unnecessary. These are universal laws.

2.31.                    This first aspect called Yama is unaffected by time, environment, class, and intelligence.

2.32.                    The second aspect is Niyama. This is Cleanliness and purity, contentment, deep spiritual faith, spiritual practice and surrender to god.

2.33.                    Reflect upon the positive aspect of existence to remove the negative.

2.34.                    Violence in thought, word or action, desire, greed, lust and anger, illusion, obsession, suffering and fear all rise from ignorance and lack of true intellectual awareness. They may affect us intensely, mildly or somewhere in-between and will continue to do this until intervention takes place. Then positive uplifting states can be felt.

2.35.                    When violence and hate are removed from within the practitioner there is a passiveness that influences others to desert hostility in their presence.

2.36.                    When pure truth is developed ones words become highly potent and influential. Words manifest into reality.

2.37.                    All that is needed will come when one does not take that which is not essential or that which is not theirs to take.

2.38.                    Remaining in celibacy allows one to gain energy and vitality.

2.39.                    Non attachment allows peace and time for contemplation. Knowledge and clarity of self stemming back to birth is found.

2.40.                    Purity in body and self must be maintained. This may include non-contact with those that hinder this purity.

2.41.                    The ability to perceive ones true self manifests further when the senses are controlled and the mind and consciousness reach purity. The practitioner becomes filled with joy.

2.42.                    A deeper happiness in gained when the practitioner is content.

2.43.                    Burning zeal and faith in practice removes impurities and enables the practitioner to gain the power necessary to control the senses.

2.44.                    Self study and reflection on a desired deity brings one into union.

2.45.                    Absolute absorption in unity is accomplished though surrender to God.

2.46.                    Happiness, steadiness and firmness must be felt in the sacred postures.

2.47.                    Passiveness is necessary for endurance when this infinite art of form is practiced.

2.48.                    Then conflictions and disturbances cease.

2.49.                    With this mastered one then begins vital breath exercise to control energy flow. Retentions and motions of inhalations and exhalations are involved.

2.50.                    The 3 parts: inhalation, exhalation and the retentions are observed, the time is measured, and carefully lengthened.

2.51.                    Breath becomes stable, rhythmic and natural.

2.52.                    Then that which covers the light is destroyed.

2.53.                    The mind becomes capable of concentration.

2.54.                    One then draws inwards removing oneself from external and internal distractions. The senses will no longer obstruct one from the path.

2.55.                    Then the highest level of controlling the senses is achieved.

 

 

3.      Powers

3.1.   Concentration is focusing the mind on something and remaining fixed.

3.2.   Meditation is moving closer to this without distraction.

3.3.   Samadhi is becoming perfectly absorbed in stillness and union with that which is concentrated and meditated upon.

3.4.   The three merge into one. This is Samyama.

3.5.   When mastered a pure wisdom and insight surfaces in relation to that which has been absorbed.

3.6.   This mastery is developed gradually.

3.7.   These three aspects are internal compared with the others.

3.8.   But even this can be perceived as external in comparison to the seedless states of Samadhi.

3.9.   Thoughts rise but are suppressed. Re-occurring thoughts and deeper impressions are also supressed and removed.The connection is made with the moment between. This is transformation through restraint.

3.10.                    Continual peace flows when these rising impressions are suppressed.

3.11.                    The many scattered thoughts slowly disappear, a one pointed mind emerges. Consciousness is transforming into spiritual oneness with the source of life.

3.12.                    Again there is a rise and fall in and out of this state. It is similar to before. Action must be taken for consciousness to remain one pointed and for transformation to continue.

3.13.                    Then a visible transformation occurs in body and senses, character, behavior and actions.

3.14.                    Calmness is present in being but true potential has not yet manifested. The virtuous road must be closely followed and proper action followed.

3.15.                    Proceeding will cause transformations that are quite apparent.

3.16.                    Knowledge of the past and future comes from the transformation caused by the merging of concentration, meditation and Samadhi.

3.17.                    Sound, meaning and feelings merge. Then the universal language of all beings is known.

3.18.                    Hidden and past impressions surface into consciousness. Everything about them becomes known. They may come from experiences in life or beyond birth.

3.19.                    Consciousness of others is known.

3.20.                    This knowledge is not to be used unless aiding others in finding that which they cannot perceive but seek in life.

3.21.                    Control of the energy form in the body leads to the power to change the effects light has on it. When light does not interact with form it becomes invisible to the eye.

3.22.                    By the same means sound is controlled.

3.23.                    Understand and master all actions. Know their effects which can be immediate or may remain dormant surfacing later. Time of death can known from this knowledge or from omens.

3.24.                    Inner strength manifests from the cultivation of friendliness.

3.25.                    Samyama on strength allows its growth to that of an elephant.

3.26.                    This knowledge of undetectable light close and far becomes known.

3.27.                    Samyama on the sun brings knowledge of deeper worlds.

3.28.                    Samyama on the moon brings knowledge of the order of the stars.

3.29.                    Samyama on the North Star brings knowledge of destiny.

3.30.                    Samyama on the navel brings knowledge of energy centers and their interactions.

3.31.                    Samyama on the hollow in the throat brings control over hunger and thirst.

3.32.                    Samyama on the tortoise energy line at the base of the skull gives firmness and steadiness.

3.33.                    Samyama on the light in the head brings the ability to perceive evolved beings.

3.34.                    Pure illuminated spiritual perception leads to knowledge of all there is to be known.

3.35.                    Samyama on the heart leads to knowledge of consciousness.

3.36.                    Understanding of soul in its pure luminosity becomes distinct and apart from the awareness and experience of pain and pleasure and our interest in it. Restraint must once again be exercised to gain this knowledge of soul.

3.37.                    Then spiritual perception in hearing and sound, touch, sight, taste and smell become enhanced.

3.38.                    Powers that rise can be followed but will only act as obstacles in breaking down seperation and becoming completely absorbed in the source of life.

3.39.                    Relaxing the forces that bond one to the body will allow the consciousness to enter the body of others.

3.40.                    Mastery of one of the five pranas (udana) moves energy from the lower body upward creating a lightness and ascension that allows one to move over water, mud, thorns and more without contact.

3.41.                    Mastery of the vital energy involved in gastric fire creates a aura that glows like a blazing fire.

3.42.                    Samyama on the relationship between space and sound. By restraining hearing one learns to hear divine sounds.

3.43.                    Relationship between the physical body and body of ether is known. This physical body can be constrained in order for the body of ether to become light like a cotton fiber. It can then move through space.

3.44.                    The veil of energy obstruction caused by the perception of separation dissolves completely. Consciousness can now move beyond the body.

3.45.                    Samyama on gross and light forms leads to knowledge of their purpose. In restraining the gross bodies interactions in certain ways a mastery is gained over the elements.

3.46.                    From this perfection of the body manifests, obstructions disappear. More powers surface.

3.47.                    A graceful attractive form, charm, beauty, great strength and firmness is perfection in body.

3.48.                    The power of understanding true form. The need for restraint in the ego and the senses yet understanding their purpose. This is the conquest.

3.49.                    Then a sharpness of mind comes due to it unbinding of the senses and their not interfering with the purity if spirit in expression.

3.50.                    When knowledge has been successfully understood and the necessary discrimination applied a pure illuminative state of the soul manifests. All is then known.

3.51.                    In destroying the seeds of bondage and being unattached to the powers that manifest we can progress to the ultimate emancipated states of Yoga.

3.52.                    Summons to respectful status and higher ranks manifests as a result of the knowledge of samyama. This may stimulate surprise and joy. An effort must be made to still any attachment.

3.53.                    Samyama on sequences of events in relation to time manifests knowledge.

3.54.                    Then knowledge comes regarding that which seems to be of similar origin and form but has hidden properties that differentiate it.

3.55.                    In this way all is known even that which is unrelated and unaffected by time.

3.56.                    Perfect freedom occurs when pure consciousness and the purity of soul become equal.

 

 

4.      Emancipation

4.1.   One may gain insightful knowledge from birth, herbs, drugs, intense spiritual practice, or profound meditation.

4.2.   A new state of life comes from the transformation in yoga. Nature's energy flows though us in abundance.

4.3.   This is a more subtle use of nature than that of a agriculturist, but there are also many similarities.

4.4.   Alone in our individuality we can measure and extend the auras of consciousness.

4.5.   Consciousness can move in many ways, but when centered to oneness, it becomes influential in progression.

4.6.   Freedom from unnecessary influence and impressions comes as a result of meditation.

4.7.   A yogis actions are neither white nor black. Others fall under 3 tendencies.

4.8.   Impressions corresponding to these tendencies manifest into their developed states.

4.9.   Past impressions and memories reveal the essence of origin and position in relation to time. There are similarities for each lifetime.

4.10.                    Some of those memories and impressions are without beginning. They have been with us eternally.

4.11.                    There are causes of memories and impressions. Effects which are reliant and bound to causes become absent and disappear without them.

4.12.                    Both past and future are true. One has occurred the other is to occur. That which has occurred characterizes that which is yet to occur.

4.13.                    They manifest changes in the subtle and gross qualities of nature.

4.14.                    Objects in nature remain real, but undergo changes due to the awareness of oneness caused by transformation.

4.15.                    Objects appear similar, but each consciousness perceives them differently.

4.16.                    An object remains to be an object whether perceived or not perceived.

4.17.                    Objects remain unknown until consciousness becomes fully aware of them. Then they are known.

4.18.                    The soul and its source become always known. No longer lost in the fluctuations of consciousness.

4.19.                    Consciousness cannot illuminate the self and perceive life simultaneously.

4.20.                    Perceiving and comprehending do not occur in the same instant.

4.21.                    When the mind is overloaded, there is confusion. An inability to properly use memory and consciousness occurs.

4.22.                    Consciousness assumes its own form and reflects intelligence when the perceiver is accomplished in mind stillness.

4.23.                    Consciousness is the source of all comprehension for the seer. It is influenced and colored by that which is seen in the external.

4.24.                    Impressions, memories, desires and immeasurable thoughts are tangled and woven into cosciousness.

4.25.                    There is growth in the distinction between the soul and that which perceives. The perception created by ego dissolves.

4.26.                    Then streams of intelligence appear in consciousness regarding the invisible existence. Consciousness must draw closer to this knowledge.

4.27.                    Move confidently towards openings in the space between impressions.

4.28.                    Then it is said that our afflictions must be abandoned.

4.29.                    Profound meditation will set us free from desires and distractions with the proper use of discriminative thinking. Then supreme intelligence rains upon us. We now have the knowledge necessary to remain in union with the source of life.

4.30.                    The afflictions of Karma cease to affect us.

4.31.                    Impurities are removed and shrouds concealing infinite universal knowledge vanish. This newly found knowledge and wisdom is glorious. All that was learned previously seems insignificant.

4.32.                    All that has to be done in order to remove the perception of separation and become transformed into illumination has been done. That which is external can no longer affect us.

4.33.                    Approaching the end of the journey, moments of interruption cease, in due course, illuminative states of transformation and expansion prevail.

4.34.                    Universal requirements of life have been achieved and moved aside or discarded, the qualities of nature no longer draw the seer, all can be left behind as movement is made back into the purity of consciousness experienced before birth, a beautiful liberation overcomes our essence and a pure consciousness is firmly established with life's source.
